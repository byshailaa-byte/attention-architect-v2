# The 4-Axis Consumer Framework
## How the 6 validated dimensions map to Direction / Stability / Resistance / Recovery

---

## 1. The mapping, finalized

The 4 axes tell a sequential story — where attention goes, whether it holds, whether it survives competition, and whether it comes back after depletion. This sequence is also the report's natural narrative arc.

| Axis | Question it answers | Primary source dimension(s) | Drives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | Where does {name}'s attention naturally go, and why? | `attention_shape` + `reward_driver` | The child's Attention Type (archetype) — this axis *is* the archetype |
| **Stability** | Once attention goes there, does it hold? | `friction_response` (does difficulty break the pattern) + `attention_shape` (narrow-deep types hold by default; wide-shifting/sensation-seeking types are naturally more variable) | How "reliable" the pattern feels day to day — this is what parents mean by "why is it different every day" |
| **Resistance** | Can it survive something more compelling nearby? | `attention_competition` (what's pulling from outside) + the avoid/derail ends of `friction_response` (what's pulling from inside) | Explains screen-time battles and distraction specifically — the axis most parents arrived at the assessment already worried about |
| **Recovery** | Does it come back after being spent? | `recharge_type` | Explains why the same child is "great in the morning, gone by evening" or vice versa |

**What stays outside the 4-axis model entirely:** `parent_instinct` does not become a 5th axis and does not get folded into any of the 4. It remains the separate **Parent Pattern** label (Quick Fixer / Pusher / Negotiator / Steady Hand), exactly as locked earlier. This preserves the two-label structure — Child's Direction/Stability/Resistance/Recovery profile, plus the Parent's Pattern — which is what makes the *combination* presentable as a single clean idea instead of five overlapping scores.

---

## 2. Why this specific reasoning for "Stability" (the one axis without an obvious 1:1 source)

Direction, Resistance, and Recovery each map cleanly to one dominant dimension. Stability doesn't, because "does the pattern hold" isn't really its own measured construct — it's a *behavior of the other dimensions under normal (non-competing) conditions.* A child whose attention is Narrow-Deep and rarely derails under friction will look highly stable — locked into a groove once they start. A child who is Sensation-Seeking and derails easily under friction will look highly variable — brilliant one day, unreachable the next, for reasons that look inconsistent to a parent but are actually a coherent pattern once named.

This matters for the report specifically: **"Stability" is the axis that explains the single most common parent complaint we haven't directly addressed yet** — *"I don't understand why it works some days and not others."* That's not a 5th mystery dimension; it's Direction and Friction Response interacting, and naming that interaction as "Stability" gives parents a word for something they already feel but couldn't previously name. That's a real "how did it know that" moment, which is exactly the recognition beat the report needs early.

## 3. Outcome language mapping (for report copy and ad copy — not for scoring)

Each axis translates to the outcome language parents actually search for and respond to. This is a translation layer only — the underlying dimension names stay in the data model and the validation paper; these labels are what parents see.

| Axis | Parent-facing outcome language |
|---|---|
| Direction | "Helping {name} focus on what actually matters to them" / "Increased engagement" |
| Stability | "More consistent focus, day to day" / "Fewer unpredictable good-day-bad-day swings" |
| Resistance | "Reduced screen battles" / "Better resistance to distraction" / "Increased attention span" |
| Recovery | "Better recovery after a demanding day" / "More sustainable focus without burnout" |

"Attention alignment" (is their attention currently pointed at what matters) and "attention compounding" (is the pattern building toward something over time, or stuck) are **narrative frames used in report copy**, not additional scored dimensions — they're the "so what" sentences that follow the 4-axis data, e.g.: *"Right now, {name}'s Direction and Resistance are working against each other — that's the compounding problem. Fix the interaction, and both start working for {name} instead."*

## 4. The combination as the flagship selling point — concrete structure

This is the piece you specifically flagged as a top selling point, so it needs its own clear moment in the report, not just an implication. Proposed structure for where this lands (Section 2 or a new dedicated section):

> **{Child}'s Direction: [Archetype]. Your Pattern: [Parent Pattern].**
> **Here's what that combination actually means for the two of you.**

Followed by a short, specific paragraph naming the *interaction*, not just each piece separately — e.g.: *"The Storm needs to feel ownership over a challenge before they'll engage with it. The Pusher's instinct is to lean in harder when engagement drops. Put together, this is likely the exact loop behind your homework battles: the more you push, the more {name} needs to feel it's theirs, which reads as resistance, which invites more pushing."*

This single paragraph — the *named interaction*, not either trait alone — is the sentence a parent screenshots. It's also the thing no competitor can replicate without also measuring the parent, which is the actual moat.

---

## 5. What this means for the existing question bank

Good news: because the 6 real dimensions haven't changed, **the question bank we already wrote for those 6 dimensions doesn't need to be rewritten.** What changes is:

1. **Report and marketing copy** now groups and labels results using Direction/Stability/Resistance/Recovery + Parent Pattern, instead of surfacing dimension names like `attention_shape` or `friction_response` directly to the parent.
2. **The result screen** (from the interactive mockup we built earlier) needs a new "translation" layer showing the 4 axes + parent pattern, rather than showing raw dimension pills.
3. **Scoring stays identical** — attention_shape + reward_driver still determine the archetype; parent_instinct still determines the pattern; the other three dimensions still feed report depth. The 4-axis model is a presentation lens on the same underlying computation, not a new formula.
