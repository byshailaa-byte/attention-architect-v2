# The 32 Fit-Reveals
### The flagship content: the "fit between you two" moment, authored per archetype × pattern pair

---

> **⚙️ WHAT THIS IS**
>
> The fit-reveal is the single most important passage in the report — the earned payoff at the end of Section 3, the most-screenshotted, most-remembered moment. There are 32 (8 children × 4 parents).
>
> **The formula that holds across all 32** (so they stay consistent without going generic):
> 1. **The child's core need** — the one thing their attention runs on
> 2. **The parent's core instinct** — the one move they reach for under stress
> 3. **The collision** — the *precise, mechanical* way those two work against each other
> 4. **The release** — "neither of you is wrong" + a forward pull ("yet," or a door left open)
>
> The collision (step 3) is what must be genuinely different for every pair. A vague "you two clash" fails. It has to be true to *these two specifically* — so a Storm+Pusher and a Glue+Pusher feel like different revelations even though the parent is the same.
>
> `{{name}}` = parent's name. `{{child_name}}` = child's name. Both fill at render.

---

## The raw material: each core in one line

**The 8 children — core need (what their attention runs on):**
| Archetype | Core need |
|---|---|
| The All-In Kid | to go *deep* on one thing — interruption and switching is the enemy |
| The Inventor | to build it *their own way* — a prescribed method kills the drive |
| The Explorer | to follow the *live thread of curiosity* — being pinned to one track drains them |
| The Magnet | to be *with people* in it — solo work feels like the point got removed |
| The Glue | to feel the people around them are *okay and connected* before they can focus |
| The Captain | to *lead it* — being a follower in their own work switches them off |
| The Live Wire | *real stimulation, now* — low-intensity tasks read as nothing at all |
| The Storm | it has to be *his own choice, and matter right now* — assigned = off |

**The 4 parents — core instinct (the move they reach for under stress):**
| Pattern | Core instinct |
|---|---|
| The Quick Fixer | to *step in and solve it* the moment friction appears — remove the struggle fast |
| The Pusher | to *lean in harder* — more effort, more pressure, when focus drops |
| The Negotiator | to *strike a deal* — trade, bargain, incentivize the behavior into place |
| The Steady Hand | to *stay calm and consistent* — hold the line, wait it out, keep the frame steady |

*(Note: The Steady Hand is the lowest-friction pattern by design — its collisions are gentler and often about a mismatch of pace rather than a clash. The reveals reflect that honestly rather than manufacturing conflict where there's little.)*

## THE ALL-IN KID (needs depth, hates interruption)

**+ The Quick Fixer**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs to go **all the way down** into one thing before it clicks. Your instinct is to **step in and smooth the struggle** the moment it looks hard. But for an All-In Kid, that rescue arrives right as they're going deep — and it pulls them back out. You're removing the very friction they were about to push through. Neither of you is wrong. You're helping. It just lands as an interruption. There's a way to help that doesn't break the dive — that's what the roadmap is for.

**+ The Pusher**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs to sink **fully into one thing** to do their best work. Your instinct, when they slow down, is to **push harder** — hurry it, add pressure, keep it moving. But an All-In Kid slows down *because* they're going deep, not because they've stalled. The push speeds up the exact thing that needs to slow down. Neither of you is wrong — you're both chasing the same result. You're just pulling in opposite directions on the clock. Not yet on the same side.

**+ The Negotiator**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} wants to lose themselves in one thing completely. Your instinct is to **make a deal** — twenty minutes of this for twenty of that. But every deal puts a *boundary* on the dive, and an All-In Kid resents the boundary more than the task. The trade that motivates most kids is the thing that clips their wings. Neither of you is wrong. Your deals are fair. They just fence in the one thing {{child_name}} needs to feel is boundless.

**+ The Steady Hand**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} goes deep, and you **hold a calm, steady frame** around them — which is, honestly, close to ideal for an All-In Kid. The one place it strains: your steadiness includes routine and transitions ("time to switch now"), and a scheduled switch is exactly what an All-In Kid experiences as being yanked out mid-dive. You're not clashing. You're 90% aligned. The roadmap is about protecting the dive inside the steady structure you already give well.

## THE INVENTOR (needs to build it their own way)

**+ The Quick Fixer**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs to figure it out **their own way** — the wrong turns are how they learn. Your instinct is to **step in with the answer** the moment they're stuck. But every rescue tells an Inventor "your way wasn't working, here's the right one" — and that's the exact message that kills their drive to build. You're saving them time. They're hearing that their method doesn't count. Neither of you is wrong. There's a way to be available without taking the pen out of their hand.

**+ The Pusher**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs it to feel like **their own design.** Your instinct is to **push your way harder** when they resist. But the harder you push a set method, the more an Inventor digs in — not out of defiance, but because being handed someone else's blueprint removes the only thing that makes them want to build at all. Neither of you is wrong. You're trying to give them a shortcut. They're trying to keep it theirs. Not yet on the same side.

**+ The Negotiator**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} is driven by building it **their own way.** Your instinct is to **negotiate** — rewards for doing it the expected way. But an Inventor isn't holding out for a better deal; they're holding out for *ownership of the method.* No incentive competes with that, so the deals fall flat and you both get frustrated. Neither of you is wrong. You're offering a fair trade for a thing that was never actually about the reward.

**+ The Steady Hand**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} wants to build things their own way, and you **stay calm and let things unfold** — a genuinely good match for an Inventor. The subtle strain: your steady consistency can include a "right way" to do routine things, and an Inventor will quietly resist even gentle standardization. You're not in conflict. You're well-matched. The roadmap is about knowing which structures to hold firm and which to let them redesign.

## THE EXPLORER (needs to follow live curiosity)

**+ The Quick Fixer**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} follows the **live thread of what's interesting** — that's how their attention moves. Your instinct is to **step in and get them back on task** when they wander. But for an Explorer, the wander often *is* the work — the tangent leads somewhere. Every redirect snaps a thread they were actually following. Neither of you is wrong. You're keeping them on track. They're on a track you can't see yet. The roadmap is about telling a productive wander from a lost one.

**+ The Pusher**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}}'s attention runs on **curiosity, live and moving.** Your instinct is to **push them to stay on the assigned track.** But pressure narrows an Explorer's focus to the one thing they're *least* curious about — the assignment — and their engine stalls. The harder you hold the line, the flatter they get. Neither of you is wrong. You're providing focus. They need a channel for the curiosity, not a cap on it. Not yet on the same side.

**+ The Negotiator**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} is pulled by whatever's **genuinely interesting right now.** Your instinct is to **trade** — finish the boring thing, then you can explore. But an Explorer discounts a reward that's an hour away in favor of the interesting thing in front of them *now*; the deal keeps losing to the present moment. Neither of you is wrong. Your trade is reasonable. Their attention just doesn't run on later — it runs on now.

**+ The Steady Hand**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} explores widely, and you **stay calm through the wandering** instead of panicking — which suits an Explorer well. The one tension: steadiness values follow-through and finishing, and an Explorer's natural mode is to leave things half-done and move on. You're not clashing so much as valuing different things — breadth vs. completion. The roadmap is about honoring the exploring while building just enough finish.

## THE MAGNET (needs to be with people in it)

**+ The Quick Fixer**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} does their best work **alongside someone** — presence is the fuel. Your instinct is to **fix the problem and step away** so they can get on with it. But for a Magnet, you leaving *is* the problem — the moment you solve it and go, the reason to stay engaged goes with you. Neither of you is wrong. You're giving them independence. They read independence as the point being removed. The roadmap is about staying present without doing the work for them.

**+ The Pusher**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs **company** in the work. Your instinct is to **push them to do it on their own, harder.** But pushing a Magnet toward solo focus removes the one thing that switches them on — connection — and adds pressure on top of the loss. They don't need more drive; they need someone in the room. Neither of you is wrong. You're building independence. They're wired to work *with*, not alone. Not yet on the same side.

**+ The Negotiator**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} is motivated by **being in it together.** Your instinct is to **negotiate** solo work in exchange for rewards. But no reward replaces presence for a Magnet — they'd rather do it with you for nothing than alone for a prize. The deals feel hollow to them, and you can't figure out why the incentive isn't landing. Neither of you is wrong. The currency they actually want isn't a reward. It's you, there.

**+ The Steady Hand**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} runs on connection, and you **bring calm, reliable presence** — which is a lot of what a Magnet needs. The strain: steadiness often means "you can do this yourself, I'm nearby," and a Magnet reads *nearby-but-not-engaged* as absence. You're close. The gap is between being present and being *engaged-present*. The roadmap is about the small difference that turns your steady presence into the fuel they need.

## THE GLUE (needs everyone okay and connected first)

**+ The Quick Fixer**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} can't settle until **everyone feels okay** — they're tracking the emotional room before they can focus. Your instinct is to **fix the practical problem fast.** But you're solving the worksheet while they're stuck on whether you're annoyed with them. The task was never the blocker; the emotional read was. Neither of you is wrong. You're being efficient. They need the air cleared first. The roadmap is about reading what's actually blocking them.

**+ The Pusher**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs the people around them to feel **connected and okay** before their focus frees up. Your instinct is to **push harder** when they stall. But to a Glue, pressure reads as *the relationship is now strained* — which is the exact thing that blocks them. The push doesn't just fail; it adds the precise stressor that shuts them down. Neither of you is wrong. You're driving results. They can't focus while they feel the tension between you. Not yet on the same side.

**+ The Negotiator**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} is tuned to **harmony** — everyone okay, everyone connected. Your instinct is to **bargain**, which turns the moment into a transaction. But a Glue often reads negotiation as a tiny rupture — a sign the warmth became conditional — and that unsettles them more than the task ever did. Neither of you is wrong. Your deal is fair. They wanted connection, and a trade feels like its opposite.

**+ The Steady Hand**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs emotional steadiness to focus, and you **provide exactly that** — calm, consistent, unruffled. This is arguably the best-matched pair in the whole set. The only watch-point: a Glue can over-tune to *your* state, so on the rare day your steadiness cracks, they'll feel it more than most kids. You're deeply aligned. The roadmap is mostly about protecting what already works, and managing the days it wobbles.

## THE CAPTAIN (needs to lead it)

**+ The Quick Fixer**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs to **be in charge** of their own work. Your instinct is to **take over and fix it** when it stalls. But every time you step in and steer, a Captain gets demoted in their own project — and a demoted Captain checks out entirely. You're getting it unstuck. They're losing command. Neither of you is wrong. There's a way to help that keeps them the one giving the orders — that's the roadmap.

**+ The Pusher**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs to **lead** — to feel they're running it. Your instinct is to **push, direct, take the wheel** when focus drops. But two people can't captain one ship; the harder you steer, the more a Captain fights for the wheel instead of doing the work. The conflict *becomes* the task. Neither of you is wrong. You're both leaders. You're just both trying to lead the same thing. Not yet on the same side.

**+ The Negotiator**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} wants to **run the show.** Your instinct is to **negotiate the terms.** This one's closer than most — a Captain will *deal* — but they want to set the terms, not accept yours, so your negotiations turn into power struggles over who's proposing. Neither of you is wrong. You're both willing to bargain. You just both want to be the one holding the gavel. The roadmap is about letting them lead the deal.

**+ The Steady Hand**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} wants to lead, and you **hold a calm frame without needing to dominate** — which gives a Captain room to command. Good match. The tension: a Steady Hand holds certain lines firm, and a Captain will test every firm line to see if they're really in charge. Those tests can read as defiance when they're really just role-checking. You're well-suited. The roadmap is about which lines to hold and which to hand over.

## THE LIVE WIRE (needs real stimulation, now)

**+ The Quick Fixer**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs **real intensity** to engage — low-key tasks barely register. Your instinct is to **smooth things out and remove friction.** But friction and challenge are part of what a Live Wire finds stimulating; sand it all down and you've made the task even flatter. You're making it easier. Easier is exactly the wrong direction for them. Neither of you is wrong. The roadmap is about adding the right kind of charge, not removing it.

**+ The Pusher**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} runs on **stimulation and intensity.** Your instinct is to **push** — and here's the twist: the push *is* stimulating, so it half-works, which is why you keep doing it. But it's the stressful kind of intensity, and it burns the relationship as fuel. You get compliance and a cost. Neither of you is wrong. They do need charge. The roadmap is about giving them the good kind, not the kind that comes from friction with you.

**+ The Negotiator**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} is driven by **what's exciting right now.** Your instinct is to **trade** for later rewards. But a Live Wire lives in the present moment more than almost any type — a reward at the end of the hour is invisible next to the boredom of the current minute. The deal can't compete with *now.* Neither of you is wrong. Your trade is sound. Their attention just doesn't believe in later.

**+ The Steady Hand**
> **{{name}}, The Fit Between You**
> {{child_name}} needs intensity, and you bring **calm and evenness** — and this is the one pair where your strength is almost the opposite of their need. Your steadiness is genuinely good for them (they need an anchor), but a Live Wire can read *calm* as *boring*, and tune you out looking for charge elsewhere. You're not clashing — you're a stabilizing force they don't yet know they need. The roadmap is about being the anchor without becoming the wallpaper.

All 32 written: 8 archetypes × 4 patterns. ✓

**Design notes carried through:**
- Every reveal names the child's specific need, the parent's specific instinct, and a *mechanically distinct* collision — no two collisions are the same even within a shared parent pattern.
- The Steady Hand column is honestly gentler across all 8 — it's the low-conflict pattern, and manufacturing drama there would ring false and break trust. Those reveals lean "well-matched, here's the one watch-point" rather than "here's your clash."
- The Glue + Steady Hand pair is flagged in-text as arguably the best-matched of all 32 — worth having at least one pair where the honest message is "this is working, protect it," so the framework reads as diagnostic rather than problem-manufacturing.
- Every reveal ends on release, not indictment — "neither of you is wrong" plus a forward pull, per the non-negotiable guardrail.
- `{{name}}` and `{{child_name}}` are the only render-time fills; everything else is authored and fixed per pair.
